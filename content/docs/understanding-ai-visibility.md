Use Cheap Seats to learn how assistants answer your buyers' questions, whether your product fits those answers, and which truthful changes are worth testing. Start with a decision you need to make, rather than a target percentage.

## What AEO and GEO mean

**AEO** means *answer engine optimization*: helping people find useful, accurate answers that can include your business. **GEO** means *generative engine optimization*: a similar goal for answers generated by AI systems. People use these terms with overlapping meanings; neither names a standardized score or a guaranteed way to get recommended.

**SEO** concerns visibility in search engines. Crawlable pages, clear explanations and credible evidence can serve both search and AI discovery. Publishing a special file or adding a phrase does not guarantee that an assistant will find, cite, understand or recommend you.

## What Cheap Seats can tell you

Cheap Seats runs your chosen questions through configured Claude and OpenAI models. A separate grader reads each saved answer. Search answers must use web retrieval; recall answers use no tools. This is a repeatable measurement of those configured systems and questions, rather than a reproduction of the ChatGPT website or every AI search surface. [Engine coverage](../engines/) lists the boundaries.

| Observation | What it tells you | What to investigate next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mentioned, recommended or absent | How this answer treats your intended product | Read the answer and the grading evidence |
| A returned URL | The search tool returned that URL | Check its title, the actual query and competing results |
| A citation | The answer links that source | Read the claim around the link and inspect the source |
| An objection | A reason the answer hesitates or prefers something else | Check whether it is accurate and relevant to the buyer |
| A change across comparable runs | The measured answers changed, with uncertainty | Check sample counts, method changes and alternative explanations |

A returned URL does not establish that the whole page was read. A citation does not establish endorsement. An absent brand does not establish a missing page or a broken product.

**Named rate** combines answers with substantive information about your brand: mentioned and recommended. It excludes namesakes and name-only disclaimers. Whether a claim is factually correct is a separate question from whether the answer recommends the product.

## Two kinds of prompt

A **buyer question** is a tracked question such as “Which tracker suits a small team that wants to self-host?” It lives in your prompt sets. **Grading instructions** tell the grader how to label an answer. Changing the buyer question changes what you measure; changing grading instructions changes how saved answers are interpreted. [Choose good questions](../choosing-questions/) before experimenting with the grader.

Other useful terms:

| Term | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| Run | One collection over a prompt set, using the available answerers, enabled tracks and samples |
| Sample | Another answer to the same question on a track; it measures variation, not another buyer need |
| Method | How answers are asked, searched and graded; a method change can break comparisons with earlier runs |
| Baseline run | Your saved starting measurement, with its questions and configuration |
| Trend | Comparable measurements over time; edited methods or questions can limit comparisons |
| Evaluation | A check of the questions, labels, evidence or a proposed change; [choose the right one](../checks-and-evaluations/) |

## Your first useful cycle

1. **Choose one buyer decision.** For example: whether teams with a technical owner can understand the operating costs of self-hosting.
2. **Write a small, coherent question set.** Include real needs and constraints. Keep questions that might reveal a legitimate reason to choose another product.
3. **Run it and read the evidence.** Review prices and budget first. Open complete answers, including ones that look favorable. [Your first run](../first-run/) shows the path.
4. **Choose an action, investigation or decision to leave things alone.** Next steps verifies proposed work against current artifacts and your stated goals. Zero actions can be useful.
5. **Check the work, then measure later.** Verify the page or product change itself. Compare later answers under suitable conditions; [measuring success](../measuring-success/) keeps these two checks separate.

## What success looks like

An early success is being able to explain a real buyer's options, identify a false claim or access problem, or decide that a competitor is the better fit. Later success can include a completed, useful change and evidence of improved visibility on relevant questions. A higher rate obtained by deleting unfavorable questions does not establish an improvement.

Business outcomes—qualified visits, trials, conversations and revenue—need your own analytics and customer evidence. Cheap Seats does not automatically attribute them to an AI recommendation.
