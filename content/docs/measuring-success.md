Define success before reading the result: which buyer situation matters, what decision the evidence should change, and which outcome would justify the work. A rise in every percentage is not a useful universal goal.

## Write a small measurement brief

| Decide in advance | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer and decision | A small technical team deciding whether self-hosting a tracker is practical |
| Question set | A stable set of relevant selection and informational questions |
| Primary reading | Search-track recommendations for selection questions; inspect factual explanations separately |
| Change and delivery check | Clarify documented operating requirements; verify the published page is accurate and readable |
| Follow-up | Comparable later runs, actual eligible counts, margins and a review date |
| Guardrails | Keep legitimate disadvantages visible; don't imply consumer-app coverage or free operation |

This is an illustrative review brief, not a new config format. Store the rationale with the work's PR or decision notes.

## Run the follow-up

1. Read the action's verification and confirm the proposed change still matters. If a necessary fact is unknown, complete the investigation first.
2. Record the PR or commit against the action. Mark accepted when the work is accepted; check that the actual target artifact is live and meets its team-controlled completion criteria.
3. Run the same relevant questions over time. Check answerers, tracks, wording, intent and measurement methods before comparing.
4. Inspect changes in named and recommended rates on the target prompts, their eligible counts and uncertainty. Also inspect page retrieval/citations and the complete answers.
5. Record an interpretation and decision: keep observing, investigate a regression, try a different supported change, or stop because the decision is settled.

## What the outcome means

| Outcome | Interpretation | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pending | No later run is available | Collect a later run when appropriate |
| Too early | The available follow-up misses the elapsed-time or answer-count floor | Keep collecting useful comparable observations |
| Needs regrade | Later answers have different grading inputs/method | Preview re-grading the named eligible runs |
| Not comparable | Answers were asked differently | Start a new baseline; re-grading cannot reconstruct the unasked answer |
| Moved | A measured rate rose beyond its margin and neither fell beyond its margin | Inspect the size, answers and other possible explanations |
| Worse | A rate fell beyond its margin and neither rose beyond its margin | Check the affected questions, tradeoffs and external changes |
| Mixed | One rate rose and another fell beyond their margins | Decide which buyer outcome matters; don't collapse the tradeoff into a score |
| No detectable change | The available changes do not exclude zero within their margins | Inspect uncertainty; benefit or harm may still be undetected |

These are outcome interpretations, not evidence that the work caused the movement. Model and web changes, sampling and other work can affect the answers too. **Newly surfaced** means a target page began appearing in recorded retrieval or citations; it is separate supporting evidence, not a recommendation-rate improvement.

A margin describes uncertainty around an observed change. For an illustrative change of **+5 percentage points with a ±10-point margin**, the interval runs from −5 to +15 and includes zero. That reading cannot distinguish the change from zero; it does not establish that the true effect is zero or smaller than ten points.

## How long to wait

The app's outcome floor requires a comparable follow-up at least **seven days** after marking and at least **four post-change answers per target prompt on average**. Both matter. Neither guarantees enough precision to detect the effect you care about. Weekly collection with only one eligible answer per target can require several weeks; check the real counts for your configuration.

Use an affordable stable cadence, then increase samples only when their expected precision helps a decision. Repeating the same question measures variation; adding many paraphrases does not create broader buyer coverage. Avoid changing the measurement instrument mid-experiment to get a better number.

## When the right outcome is no action

An assistant may prefer another product for a feature you deliberately do not offer. A proposed page may already exist. A publisher may not accept the desired listing. Record the useful learning and the decision it informs; do not invent implementation work to fill the queue.

**Success:** truthful work that addresses a real buyer need, a reliable reading of the available evidence, and a decision proportionate to that evidence. If an outcome stays uncertain, say what additional evidence would change the decision—or why further measurement is not worth the cost.
